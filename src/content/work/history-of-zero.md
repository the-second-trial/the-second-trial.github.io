Zero seems obvious now, but it was one of mathematics' hardest-won ideas. For
most of history there was simply no symbol for *nothing* — and certainly no
notion that nothing could be a number you calculate with.

## Placeholder, then number

Zero began as a placeholder, a way to tell 25 from 205. Only later did
mathematicians dare to treat it as a quantity in its own right — something you
could add, subtract, and eventually divide *toward*.

## The heart of calculus

Limits, derivatives, infinitesimals — the whole edifice of calculus is built on
carefully approaching zero. Nothing, it turns out, became the foundation of
nearly everything.
