Statistics is the science of being careful about *almost*. Almost certain,
almost significant, almost a real effect. The trouble is that "almost" is
exactly where we tend to deceive ourselves.

## The p-value trap

A p-value tells you how surprising your data would be if nothing were going on.
It does **not** tell you the probability that your hypothesis is true — a
confusion that has misled a great deal of published research.

## Honest uncertainty

Confidence intervals, properly understood, are humbler and more useful: they
show the range of effects compatible with what you saw. Learning to sit with
that uncertainty is most of the discipline.
