A protein is a long chain of amino acids that, left to its own devices, folds
into a precise three-dimensional shape. That shape determines what the protein
*does* — and predicting it from the sequence alone is one of biology's hardest
problems.

## The energy landscape

Folding is a search for the lowest-energy configuration. Picture a rugged
landscape of hills and valleys; the protein rolls downhill, sometimes getting
stuck in a local minimum before finding the global one.

This toy model lets you nudge a simplified chain and watch it settle, building
intuition for why folding is both fast and occasionally goes wrong.
