Gravity, we are taught, is a force. Drop an apple and it falls because the
Earth pulls on it. But there is a stranger, more beautiful story — one in
which gravity is not a force at all, but the **shape of spacetime itself**.

## Straight lines on a curved surface

Imagine walking in a perfectly straight line across the surface of the Earth.
Keep going, and you trace out a great circle — eventually arriving back where
you started. You never turned, yet your path curved. The curvature was in the
surface, not in your choice of direction.

This is the central idea of general relativity. Massive objects curve the
spacetime around them, and everything else simply follows the straightest
possible path — a **geodesic** — through that curved geometry.

## Why the apple falls

The apple is not pulled. It is following its geodesic through a spacetime that
the Earth has gently bent. What we feel as weight is the ground pushing us off
of our natural, free-falling path.

> Spacetime tells matter how to move; matter tells spacetime how to curve.

It is less a force than a slope — and once you see it that way, gravity never
quite looks the same again.
