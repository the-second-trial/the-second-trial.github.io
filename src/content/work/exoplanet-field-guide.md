We cannot photograph most exoplanets — they are too small, too dim, and far
too close to their blinding host stars. And yet we have found thousands. How?

## The transit method

When a planet passes in front of its star, it blocks a tiny fraction of the
light. Measure that dip precisely enough and you can infer the planet's size,
its orbit, and sometimes even the composition of its atmosphere.

A light curve is a quiet thing — just a wiggle in a graph — but read carefully,
it describes a world.
